Standing up mobile research capability during a company-wide pivot.

A global services marketplace · Mobile UXR Lead
Outcome
Built the prioritization framework, testing infrastructure, and research handbook the org needed to operationalize a mobile-first strategy. Scaled mobile research capability beyond a single researcher.
Methods
Heuristic analysis · Tiered prioritization · Test infrastructure design · Process documentation
Role
Mobile UXR Lead during a company-wide mobile-first pivot. Owned the research strategy, the tooling work, and the documentation that handed capability to the rest of the org.

CONTEXT

The platform was historically desktop-first, with product development and UX optimized for the web. Internal data began to show that nearly half of user interactions were happening on mobile, often in combination with desktop. Mobile experience was meaningfully underdeveloped: two separate apps for clients and freelancers, neither fully native, with inconsistent Android support and many core flows that simply did not work on phone. Leadership declared a move to mobile-first, but did not specify what that meant in practice.

PROBLEM

This was not a single product problem. It was a company-wide capability gap. There was no clear prioritization of mobile features, no reliable understanding of mobile user behavior, no infrastructure for testing mobile experiences, and no established research workflows or tooling. The central question became how to operationalize a mobile-first strategy without the tools, processes, or clarity needed to support it.

APPROACH

Given the urgency, I prioritized speed and clarity. I started with a heuristic analysis of the key mobile flows, working with internal UX experts to evaluate onboarding, messaging, payments, and hiring-related interactions. The output was a set of color-coded, highly visual charts that showed which flows were broken, which were functional, and the severity of each issue. The artifacts were designed for rapid reading across teams.

I combined those findings with existing behavioral data and business priorities to build a tiered prioritization framework. Payment flows landed at the top despite lower frequency because of revenue impact; messaging was high-frequency and stable, so it ranked lower for redesign.

WHAT WE LEARNED

Mobile usage did not equate to full mobile capability. Users frequently switched between mobile and desktop depending on the task. Critical flows (payments, onboarding) were broken in ways that mattered. The primary constraint was infrastructure rather than design: the org could not test mobile interactions properly, especially in a multi-sided context with both clients and freelancers in the loop.

The bottleneck was not insight. It was the inability to test the insight against real users at the speed mobile-first required.

RECOMMENDATION

Beyond the prioritization framework, I worked with engineering to create test account systems that allowed realistic end-to-end mobile testing across platforms. I evaluated and integrated third-party research tools into the mobile app, enabling behavioral tracking and usability testing for the future. Finally, I authored a mobile research handbook documenting tools, processes, and best practices, so the capability could scale beyond me.

The work established the tiered roadmap, enabled mobile testing infrastructure for the first time, contributed to an organizational shift in how mobile was prioritized, and put research process into the hands of teams that had been operating without it.
