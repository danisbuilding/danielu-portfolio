Surfacing the hidden team economy inside a solo-first marketplace.

A global services marketplace · Lead UX Researcher
Outcome
Reframed agencies as marketplace multipliers. Shifted internal thinking on identity, reputation, fee structure, and team representation. Redirected several upstream product investments.
Methods
In-depth interviews · Discovery research · Marketplace systems analysis · Strategic narrative
Role
Lead UX Researcher partnering with revenue and marketplace product. Owned the discovery agenda, conducted the interviews personally, and presented the synthesis to product leadership.

CONTEXT

The marketplace's revenue model favored larger, longer-term engagements. Analysis showed those high-value projects were disproportionately complex and most often fulfilled by multi-person teams rather than individuals. The product, however, was designed almost entirely around individual freelancers. Leadership suspected that supporting "agencies" better could unlock higher-value work, but no one had a clear picture of how agencies actually operated on the platform.

PROBLEM

There was a mismatch between how work was actually getting done (team-based) and how the platform represented it (individual-based). High-value work was happening in shadow structures, fee and permission systems were creating perverse incentives, and the platform's identity model could not represent collaboration at scale. The strategic question: how do you redesign for team-based work without breaking trust or marketplace dynamics?

APPROACH

I conducted in-depth qualitative interviews with about eight high-performing agency owners across IT, design, and content. The goal was to understand real-world agency behavior at the high end, where the patterns are most informative, rather than to sample broadly. The questions probed structure, workflow, fee strategies, identity, client perception, and the workarounds owners had developed inside a platform built for individuals.

WHAT WE LEARNED

Agency models varied widely. Some were structured firms with offices and fixed staff; others were loose, fluid collectives. The platform's fee structure and permission systems incentivized "shadow teams," where one freelancer fronted publicly while others worked behind the scenes. Identity and permissions were misaligned with team-based work, making collaboration hard to represent. Clients carried stigma against agencies, often assuming lower transparency or higher costs.

Agency owners were operating as high-leverage marketplace multipliers, combining domain expertise, business development, and talent vetting. The platform was treating them as enlarged individuals, and losing the value of the multiplier in the process.

RECOMMENDATION

Make team structures first-class on the platform. Enable explicit representation of collaborators. Redesign reputation systems so feedback could attach to both individuals and teams. Fix fee structures that penalized collaboration. Address client stigma through segmentation and positioning. Expand profile customization to support agency branding.

The work surfaced hidden marketplace dynamics and reframed agencies as growth drivers rather than awkward fits. It influenced internal thinking on marketplace structure, incentive design, and identity systems, and shifted several upstream investments toward supporting team-based work.
